Workplace Eye Wellness: Protecting Your Vision on the Job

Mar 04, 2025

Common Workplace Eye Issues

Long hours of screen time, poor lighting, and incorrect workstation setups can lead to several eye-related issues. Many workers experience eye fatigue, discomfort, or vision changes without realizing that their work environment is a contributing factor. Addressing these concerns can help maintain long-term eye health and productivity.

1. Digital Eye Strain

2. Dry Eyes

Staring at screens for long hours reduces blinking, which can lead to dryness, irritation, and redness. Air conditioning, office fans, and indoor heating can worsen the condition.

3. Glare & Poor Lighting

Bright overhead lights, glare from windows, and harsh screen brightness can contribute to discomfort, eye strain, and difficulty focusing.

4. Incorrect Monitor Positioning

A monitor placed too high or too low can force the eyes to work harder, leading to strain and even neck pain.

How to Protect Your Eyes at Work

Making small adjustments to your daily routine and workstation setup can significantly reduce eye strain and improve comfort. Simple changes such as proper screen positioning, adequate lighting, and frequent breaks can make a big difference in maintaining healthy vision throughout the workday.

1. Follow the 20-20-20 Rule

Reduce digital eye strain by taking breaks: Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds to relax your eyes.

2. Adjust Your Screen Settings

Reduce screen brightness to match the surrounding light.

3. Position Your Monitor Correctly

Keep your screen about an arm’s length away (20–24 inches).

If you spend long hours in front of a computer, you’ve probably experienced tired, dry eyes or blurry vision by the end of the workday. Digital screens, artificial office lighting, and prolonged focus can affect your eye health, leading to discomfort and decreased productivity. In recognition of Workplace Eye Wellness Month, Eye Shop highlights ways to keep your eyes healthy at work.

Protecting your vision is essential whether you work in an office, at home, or in an industrial setting. Let’s explore the common causes of workplace eye strain and how you can prevent them.

How to Protect Your Eyes at Work

1. Follow the 20-20-20 Rule

2. Adjust Your Screen Settings

Increase text size to avoid squinting.

Use blue light filters to reduce eye strain.

3. Position Your Monitor Correctly

Position the top of the screen at or just below eye level.

Tilt your screen slightly to reduce glare.

4. Reduce Office Glare

Adjust window blinds to minimize glare.

Use anti-glare screens on monitors.

Choose matte screen monitors instead of glossy ones.

5. Prevent Dry Eyes

Blink more often to keep your eyes moist.

Use artificial tears if your eyes feel dry.

Stay hydrated to support natural tear production.

6. Improve Office Lighting

Avoid harsh overhead lights—use softer, indirect lighting when possible.

Position your computer screen away from direct light sources.

Use desk lamps with adjustable brightness.

7. Wear Proper Eyewear

If you wear glasses or contact lenses , ensure your prescription is up to date. Blue light-blocking lenses or anti-reflective coatings can help reduce strain for those who spend hours in front of a screen.
