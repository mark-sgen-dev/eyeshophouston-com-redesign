Dry Eye Prevention Tips for Long-Term Comfort

Jul 04, 2025

Can Dry Eye be Prevented?

Dry eye disease doesn’t always show up out of nowhere. It’s often the result of environmental stressors, lifestyle habits, and even minor oversights in daily routines. While certain medical conditions, like meibomian gland dysfunction, can make dry eye harder to avoid, many cases can be prevented—or at least improved—by paying closer attention to what your eyes go through each day.

Top 6 Ways to Prevent Dry Eye Disease

Let’s face it—your eyes do a lot of heavy lifting. From early morning phone checks to late-night streaming, they rarely catch a break. These tips are easy to build into your day and can help prevent dryness before it starts.

1. Stay Hydrated

Your tear film is mostly water, and if your body is dehydrated, your eyes will feel it first. Sip water consistently throughout the day, rather than chugging it all at once.

2. Adjust Your Screen Habits

Blinking slows way down when we’re focused on screens. Take short breaks every 20 minutes to blink fully, and keep screens at eye level to reduce strain.

3. Shield Your Eyes from Airflow

Dry indoor air from A/C, heaters, and fans can quickly dry out your eyes. Try redirecting vents in your car and at your desk so they don’t blow directly at your face.

4. Use Proper Eye Makeup Techniques

Makeup applied too close to the lash line can block the oil glands that support healthy tears, making your symptoms worse. Always remove your eye makeup gently and thoroughly before going to bed.

5. Choose Protective Eyewear

Wind, dust, and sun exposure are all triggers for dry eye. Wear wraparound sunglasses or glasses with side shields that can help reduce exposure outdoors.

6. Know When It’s Time for Treatment

If you’re already using over-the-counter eye drops more than twice a day or still experience the gritty and uncomfortable symptoms of dry eye, it’s time for a professional evaluation.
