Healthy Aging Eyes: What to Know About Presbyopia

Sep 25, 2026

Why Vision Changes With Age

A flexible natural lens inside each eye helps you shift focus between different distances. With age, this lens becomes less flexible, making nearby words and objects harder to focus on. This common change is called presbyopia and often becomes noticeable in your 40s.

The Top Signs of Presbyopia to Look Out For

Presbyopia usually develops slowly. At first, you may change small habits without realizing your near vision is behind them. Watch for these common signs as you move through your 40s and beyond.

Your Comfortable Reading Distance Changes

You may start moving your phone, book, or tablet farther from your face to bring the words into focus. That extra distance can make reading easier as your eyes lose some of their close-focusing ability.

Dim Lighting Makes Reading Harder

A menu in a softly lit restaurant or a label in a dark room may suddenly require more effort. Brighter lighting can make close-up details easier to see, so that you may reach for a lamp or your phone's flashlight more often.

Screens Feel Different by the End of the Day

Hours spent switching between a computer, phone, and paperwork can make changes in near vision easier to notice. Words may seem less crisp later in the day, or your eyes may feel tired after extended close work.

Fine Details Require More Effort

Threading a needle, checking a price tag, reading directions, or working on a favorite hobby may feel harder than it used to. Presbyopia can become especially noticeable during tasks that depend on seeing small details clearly.

Close Work May Trigger Headaches

Trying to maintain clear near focus for long periods can sometimes contribute to headaches or eye strain. If close work has become uncomfortable, an eye exam can help our eye doctors determine whether a change in your vision correction could help.

Presbyopia FAQs

Is Presbyopia a Normal Part of Aging?

Yes. Presbyopia is a common age-related change that affects the eye’s ability to focus on nearby objects. Most people begin noticing changes in their 40s.

Will Presbyopia Keep Changing?

Near vision can keep changing over time. Regular eye exams let our eye doctors monitor your vision and update your prescription as needed.

Do I Need an Eye Exam for Reading Glasses?

An eye exam can identify your exact prescription and check your overall eye health. Our eye doctors can also recommend vision correction based on how you read, work, drive, and use digital devices.
