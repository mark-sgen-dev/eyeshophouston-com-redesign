What Are Eye Allergies?

Eye allergies, also called allergic conjunctivitis, occur when your immune system reacts to everyday triggers like pollen, dust, or pet dander. Instead of simply washing irritants away, your eyes release histamines, leading to redness, itching, and irritation. While allergies don’t usually cause permanent damage, they can mimic other conditions, which is why scheduling an eye exam in Houston is essential to rule out more serious issues.

Seasonal vs. Year-Round Allergies

Not all eye allergies are the same. Seasonal allergies, also known as hay fever, are most common in the spring and fall when pollen counts are high. They cause sudden flare-ups of itching, redness, and watery eyes. Perennial allergies, on the other hand, occur year-round and are usually triggered by indoor factors like dust mites, mold, or pet dander.

Symptoms That Point to Eye Allergies

While eye allergies are often confused with dry eye or infections, certain symptoms are more common with allergic reactions. These include red, watery eyes, burning sensations, swollen eyelids, blurred vision, and light sensitivity.

Can Allergies Affect Your Vision?

Although most people associate allergies with sneezing and congestion, they can also affect your eyesight. Swelling in the eye tissues can lead to temporary blurred vision or make it harder to focus. Constant rubbing of itchy eyes may also increase the risk of developing dry eye syndrome , which requires its own treatment plan. For patients with existing conditions like myopia or keratoconus, unmanaged allergies can make visual discomfort even worse.

FAQs About Allergies and Vision

1. How do I know if my eye irritation is from allergies or an infection?

Eye allergies usually cause redness, itching, and watery eyes in both eyes, while infections often come with thicker discharge, pain, or affect only one eye. If you’re unsure, book an exam so we can properly diagnose and treat the issue.

2. Can allergies permanently damage my eyes?

No, allergies don’t typically cause permanent eye damage. However, constant rubbing and untreated inflammation can worsen conditions like dry eye or make contact lens wear uncomfortable. Professional treatment helps prevent complications.

3. What’s the best way to manage seasonal eye allergies in Houston?

In addition to prescription eye drops, simple steps like limiting outdoor exposure during high pollen days, using air filters indoors, and wearing wraparound sunglasses can make a big difference. For contact lens wearers, switching to daily disposables often helps.
